## What the 2016 paper looks like

- **Older, topic-grouped pattern**, not the current five-subject Screening layout. Seen: current affairs (Q11–Q28), polity (about Q28 and Q47–Q76), economics and Nobel prizes (Q81–Q84), history (Q85–Q96), economy (Q97–Q108).
- Bilingual: English and Telugu in two columns.
- 150 questions in 2½ hours (the booklet instruction says so).

## Findings for Ancient History

- **No ancient history question** was seen in the history block (Q85–Q96) or elsewhere in the pages read.
- The history block leans towards **economic and trade history** (markets, revenue, traders, currency, trade).

## Implications for the notes and quiz

1. This paper gives **no evidence** of which ancient topics are repeated. Do not call any ancient topic "frequently asked".
2. Since the history block favours trade, coins, revenue and markets, the ancient notes give weight to **trade, coins, guilds, land grants and agriculture** (see `Ancient History/3 Mauryan and Gupta Empires.md`).
3. Distractors in the paper come from the **same period** (other Sultans, other trader classes). Notes use "Common confusions" tables for this reason.
4. Include some **numerical-fact** practice in quizzes.
